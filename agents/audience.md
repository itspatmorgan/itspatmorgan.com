# Audience

Who the site speaks to, what each group needs from it, and the bar the work
has to clear. Every design, copy and engineering decision serves these people.

## Who it's for

1. **Design and product leaders.** Heads of design, design managers and
   product leaders at companies that build ambitious software. They visit to
   understand how Patrick thinks and works, usually through a link someone
   shared, and they skim. They want to see real shipped product, the judgment
   behind it, and outcomes they can trust, in a couple of minutes.
2. **Designers and builders.** Peers who know Patrick from Unknown Arts,
   Design Studio, talks or the community. They come for the ideas, the tools
   and the craft, and they may stay to read or try something in the Lab.
3. **Engineers and technical collaborators.** People who look at how the work
   is built: the live demos, Design Studio, and this repository itself, which
   is public and is part of the proof that Patrick designs and builds.
4. **Future maintainers.** Patrick, who rarely reads the code, and the agents
   who work on it. They need the site to be obvious to change and hard to
   break. See `principles/engineering.md`.

## What they need

- **The point first.** A visitor who reads only the headline and looks only
  at the first visual should get the idea.
- **Real work.** Shipped interfaces, recreated faithfully, not illustrations
  of them. This is what product designers are judged on.
- **Claims they can trust.** Specific outcomes, true to what Patrick did, with
  team credit where it belongs.
- **A site that feels considered.** Fast loads, quiet motion and nothing out
  of place. The site is itself a sample of the work.

## The bar

Would the people who lead design at the best product teams look at this and
want to work with Patrick? If a page, visual or interaction would not survive
that look, it isn't done.

## Positioning

The site speaks as a practitioner sharing work and ideas, not a candidate
making a pitch. Contact links are welcome; "hire me" and availability
language are not. Community copy stays location-agnostic.

## Non-goals

- A marketing-heavy landing page. The work makes the case.
- Half-finished Lab experiments in public.
- Broad abstractions or new libraries that don't clearly cut future work.
