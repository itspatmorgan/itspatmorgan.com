# Design Principles

How to decide what visitors see and feel. These come from Patrick's writing
on Unknown Arts and from his feedback on the site's work so far. Each one
names the idea, then what it means here. When two pull against each other,
the earlier one usually wins.

For the people these serve, see `../audience.md`. For classic principle sets
behind several of these, see `references.md`.

## 1. Show the real work

Lead with real shipped interfaces, recreated faithfully and animated. Use an
illustration only where no interface exists, such as a closing lesson. Never
ship a stand-in: gray-bar thumbnails, invented documents and fake queues read
as cheap and undercut the claim. Match the real component designs and keep
content where it lives in the product. When a liberty improves the screen,
name it; don't stage product behavior that doesn't exist.

## 2. One point per surface

Every slide, card and page has a single most important thing. Clarity beats
comprehensiveness. A headline and the first visual should carry the point on
their own, and anything that doesn't serve it goes.

## 3. To emphasize something, deemphasize everything else

Warm, minimal, editorial. Neutral stages, the site's own type and color, no
bright infographic backgrounds. Guide the eye along one path. Restraint is
what makes the real work stand out.

## 4. Default it or design it

Reuse the system by default: Geist and Geist Mono, the two page widths, the
shared scene player, the card layout, the share card grid. Design something
custom only where it carries the story, and then make it excellent. A new
width, font or one-off control needs a reason.

## 5. Most advanced, yet acceptable

Push the craft (live demos, real motion, details people notice) inside
patterns visitors already understand. A case study is a familiar page with an
unusually good demo, not a new kind of page to learn.

## 6. Every word earns its place

Plain, specific and true. Sentence case headings in the body font; no
all-caps labels or eyebrows. A caption appears only when it says something
the screen and headline don't. Outcomes are concrete, and team decisions say
"we". Short values in briefs and labels; no orphaned words.

## 7. Feel is part of the design

Clicks, loads and transitions should feel instant and quiet. Route motion
stays under about 250ms; state changes cross-fade rather than blink in;
nothing jumps on load. Respect reduced motion. Treat jank as a bug, not
polish.

## 8. The whole picture on every screen

Phones get the same design at the same proportions, shown whole, with a
gentle zoom only where a step needs a closer look. Prefer no zoom to a wrong
zoom. Both themes are first-class: terminals, code and diagrams follow the
page theme.

## 9. Polish what ships, ship what's polished

Published beats perfect, but what's public should be finished. Keep
half-made experiments private, and fix the details in what's live: optical
centering, even spacing, balanced line breaks, exact values from the source.
